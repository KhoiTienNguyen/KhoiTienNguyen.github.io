The edit

Not a checklist.
A journey with a pulse.

This is not a checklist of Türkiye. It is a deliberately paced first journey: six main chapters, one credible coastal alternate, and four swaps for travellers willing to trade breadth for obsession.

Fly the long jumps. Pick up a car only where landscape rewards the freedom: Cappadocia and the southwest coast.

Istanbul

The city arrives by water

3–4 days

Build the opening around contrasts: imperial scale and neighbourhood intimacy, Byzantine stone and commuter ferries, dawn courtyards and late-night tables. The Bosphorus is not an excursion—it is the city’s organising line.

Southeast edit · 5–6 days · replaces the Aegean, Pamukkale & Lycian coast

Food, deep time,
stone horizons.

Travel Gaziantep → Şanlıurfa → Mardin as one high-contrast sequence. Gaziantep can stand alone as a two-day food-first swap, but the full trio changes the trip from sea and hiking toward food, living culture, deep antiquity and stone architecture. Safranbolu follows separately as the quieter, more compact option.

Read the room

Culture

Dress with situational awareness at active mosques; shoulders and knees covered, shoes off, voices low, prayer space unobstructed. Learn a few Turkish courtesies—merhaba, lütfen, teşekkürler—and accept tea as hospitality without feeling obliged to buy. Photograph people only after asking. Archaeological sites are not climbing frames, and rural communities are homes rather than backdrops. September remains hot: begin exposed ruins early, reserve the middle of the day for shade and meals, and let evenings run late.

Travel well

Safety

Use licensed taxis or reputable apps where available, confirm the meter, and keep valuables discreet in crowded trams and bazaars. Drive only the sections that reward it—Cappadocia and the southwest coast—and avoid night driving on unfamiliar rural roads. Carry water, offline maps and sun protection on hikes; check heat, trail and boat conditions locally. Monitor official travel advice before a southeast swap, keep well clear of border zones, and buy insurance that covers hiking and rental cars. In an emergency dial 112.
